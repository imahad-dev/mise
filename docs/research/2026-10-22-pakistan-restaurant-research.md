# Rebuild Mise around karahi, cash and chai

Mise can become a convincing Pakistani demo by changing its operating model as well as its language and currency. The core changes are: portion-priced desi menus (half/full, ½ kg/1 kg, per-piece bread); a hybrid of waiter-entered and QR orders, since waiters are cheap and plentiful; KDS stations that mirror per-station KOT printers; and a bill engine where **the sales-tax rate depends on how the guest pays**. Punjab currently taxes restaurant bills at **16% for cash and 8% for card, wallet or QR**, and Sindh and Islamabad use similar two-tier structures. The legal hazard is equally specific. Federal rules make it an offence to issue a bill with a counterfeit **FBR fiscal invoice number or QR code**, with penalties of at least **Rs 500,000**. A demo must therefore look realistic while printing nothing that resembles a fiscal invoice, and it must never claim "FBR integrated". The market is crowded and cheap. Oscar gives away POS, KDS and QR ordering and earns from card fees, and most local vendors bundle FBR/PRA/SRB integration for PKR 2,000–17,500 per branch per month. Mise's differentiator therefore cannot be "QR ordering" or "KDS". It has to be the deterministic, plain-language analytics (including aggregator versus own-channel margin) that no local vendor markets. Urdu belongs in the guest app as a Naskh-font, RTL, Latin-digit locale with human-reviewed strings. The kitchen keeps short English/Roman-Urdu dish names. WhatsApp enters only as free `wa.me` share links. Much of front-of-house practice (paper pads, naan in rounds, family halls, café token calls) is unsourced and must be checked with real owners before it is built.

## Desi restaurants sell by portion, route by station, and still run on waiters

The defining feature of a Pakistani family-restaurant menu is the portion. Karahi is sold by weight or by half/full. On delivery menus, a desi chicken karahi is listed at **Rs 1,350 per kg and Rs 1,050 per 500 g**, and raita and salad are **separately priced at Rs 90 each**. A "half karahi deal" bundles five roti, raita and salad for Rs 1,300 ([Foodpanda: Herry Khichen](https://www.foodpanda.pk/restaurant/azi5/herry-khichen); [Foodpanda: Hafiz Food Corner](https://www.foodpanda.pk/restaurant/k3mn/hafiz-food-corner-sahiwal)). Mid-range and premium houses follow the same pattern at higher prices. A Lahore mutton house lists mutton karahi **Half Rs 3,300 / Full Rs 6,600**, roghni or garlic naan at Rs 300 and raita at Rs 200 ([Khan Baba](https://khanbabapk.com/product/mutton-karahi-336090)). An Islamabad karahi chain lists chicken karahi at **Rs 2,200 per kg** and mutton at Rs 4,500 per kg ([butt-karahi.com](https://butt-karahi.com/)). BBQ is sold per piece (a chicken leg tikka at **Rs 840** at a Lahore chain) or as platters ([Foodpanda: Bundu Khan](https://www.foodpanda.pk/restaurant/u7oj/bundu-khan-restaurant-dha-z-block)). For Mise this means portion must be a first-class, priced variant of a dish, not a free-text modifier. Bread, raita, salad, water and soft drinks must be billable lines, and combos are needed.

Order routing follows the printer-per-station model. Pakistani POS vendors describe dine-in orders linked to a table and sent by item type to a KDS, a kitchen printer or a prep station: drinks to the beverage counter, grill to the grill ([Oscar POS blog](https://oscar.pk/blog/how-does-a-restaurant-pos-system-work-in-pakistan)). Generic KOT documentation describes per-item printer mapping running over the local network ([PosBytz](https://help.posbytz.com/en/article/kot-mapping-and-setup-13b87c2/)). Oscar sells its KDS alongside "automated KOT routing", which suggests that printed tickets remain normal and the KDS is an upgrade rather than a replacement ([Oscar](https://oscar.pk/solutions/free-pos)). A Mise kitchen view for Pakistan should therefore default to stations (tandoor, karahi/handi, BBQ, drinks) and offer a printable ticket fallback.

Service charge and tipping are real but uneven. Formal restaurants and hotels typically add **about 10% service charge**. Low- and mid-tier places see the bill rounded up and the change left as a tip ([Wikipedia tipping customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tipping_customs_by_country)). Restaurateurs told Dawn in 2019 that a minimum 10% tip is expected ([Dawn Images](https://images.dawn.com/news/1178376)). Service charge should therefore be a configurable percentage, off by default and 10% when on. It should appear as its own line, separate from tax, with tips recorded as optional rounding.

Takeaway ("parcel") and own-rider delivery are mainstream channels, and aggregator commission is the industry's open wound. In 2020 about 200 eateries joined an All Pakistan Restaurant Association boycott after Foodpanda raised commission **from 18% to 35%**. Restaurants wanted to deliver with their own riders and keep customer data ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/1580703)). Foodpanda remains dominant. It made its first annual profit of **€11.2M in 2024** ([TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/foodpanda-pakistans-e11-2-million-profit-milestone-or-mirage/)) and handles up to 20% of partner restaurants' food business ([Arab News](https://arabnews.com/node/2612269/amp)). Restaurants cannot leave it, but they want to know what it costs them. An order-channel tag (dine-in, parcel, own delivery, aggregator) is therefore cheap to add and feeds the most persuasive analytics story Mise can tell.

Cafés behave differently. Coffee is still niche, with Pakistan drinking about **735 cups of tea per cup of coffee** ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=203904)). Even so, third-wave cafés are spreading through Lahore, Karachi and Islamabad. Owners say cafés, unlike restaurants, encourage people to linger, and the customers are mostly students and young professionals ([The Standard](https://thestandard.com.pk/the-third-space/)). That argues for a café mode with counter ordering, pay-first and a pickup number, plus an open tab that guests can add to over a long stay. Barista and food should be separate KDS stations. No source documents token or buzzer practice, so the exact pickup mechanic needs validation.

Dayparts are shaped by regulation as well as habit. In 2026, energy-saving orders capped dine-in at **11 pm in Islamabad** ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/06/03/islamabad-revises-business-timings-again/amp/)) and Balochistan ([ARY](https://arynews.tv/new-market-restaurant-timings-announced-in-balochistan)), and set a **7–11:30 pm** restaurant window in Sindh ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/04/10/sindh-announces-new-timings-for-markets-restaurants-and-wedding-halls/amp/)). Delivery and takeaway were exempt. In Ramadan, iftar becomes the ordering spike and total transactions historically fall, while delivery grows ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/922867/age-of-tech-appetite-for-online-food-orders-continues-to-grow)). Demo data that peaks in the late evening, rises on weekends and includes an iftar spike will look right to a Pakistani owner. Lunch-heavy European curves will not.

## Payment method now decides the tax rate on every bill

Pakistan's payments landscape is cash plus a fast-growing digital layer. SBP's **Raast P2M** service accepts QR, alias, IBAN and request-to-pay. Banks had to enable it by March 2024, and payers cannot be charged for it ([SBP PSD Circular 4 of 2023](https://www.sbp.org.pk/psd/2023/C4.htm)). By FY25, **838,856 merchants** were accepting Raast QR, up from 516,317 a year earlier. Card POS reached about **196,000 terminals** handling almost 1 million card payments a day ([SBP Payment Systems Review FY25](https://www.sbp.org.pk/assets/document/publications/Annual-Payment-Systems-Review-FY25.pdf)). No source gives the split of restaurant payments by method, so the demo seed mix is a guess. A realistic method list is cash, card, wallet (JazzCash/Easypaisa), Raast QR and bank transfer, with a derived "digital" flag.

That flag matters because provincial and federal tax now depends on it:

The table has known conflicts. In July 2026 the PRA spokesman said "other provinces" charge 8%, which does not match ICT's 5% card rate. Sindh also lets some integrated restaurants keep charging 15% on digital payments under a dispensation list whose size is reported variously as 58 or 73 outlets ([Geo](https://www.geo.tv/latest/555650-karachis-58-restaurants-authorised-to-collect-nearly-double-service-tax); [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/07/18/pakistan-tax-bar-association-accuses-sindh-revenue-board-of-creating-disparity-in-digital-payment-at-restaurants)). Rates changed on 1 July in both 2025 and 2026, so Mise must store tax as data keyed by jurisdiction, payment class and effective date, never as constants. Split tenders (part cash, part card) have no documented treatment. The demo should either tax each tender portion at its own rate or allow one tender per bill, and label the choice as an assumption.

For a demo, the payment model is recording, not processing. Mise should capture method, amount, and a reference (card RRN or approval code, Raast or wallet transaction ID) or, for cash, the amount tendered and change given. That supports an end-of-day close comparing expected against counted cash and digital totals against settlement. This is standard POS practice, but no Pakistan-specific source confirms how restaurants reconcile today. Mise must not take real payments, since that would cut against both the free-only rule and the regulated nature of acquiring.

## The bill must look real but must never look fiscal

Federal rules under Sales Tax Rules Chapter XIV-A cover restaurants, cafés, coffee shops and eateries. They must install FBR-approved fiscal software, register every POS, and **show prices and tax separately on menus** (rule 150ZB(4)). Each invoice must carry a POS registration number, a sequential invoice number, date and time, item-wise price excluding tax, quantity, tax rate, discount, tax charged and mode of payment (rule 150ZEB(5)). It must also carry an **FBR fiscal invoice number and a QR code** obtained from FBR in real time (rule 150ZEB(6)). Integrated outlets display an "Integrated with FBR" signboard (rule 150ZEB(17)). Issuing an invoice without, or with a counterfeit or duplicate, fiscal number or QR code carries a penalty of **Rs 500,000 or 200% of the tax, whichever is higher**, possible imprisonment, and sealing ([FBR POS Booklet](https://download1.fbr.gov.pk/Docs/2023919991847265Point-of-Sale(POS)-Booklet-(LegalProvision)-updated-18.09.2023.pdf)). Enforcement is real: Islamabad tax officials sealed restaurants over fake receipts ([The News](https://www.thenews.com.pk/print/1253758-fbr-seals-five-restaurants-for-issuing-fake-receipts)). PRA has fined restaurants for charging tax they never declared ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2020/09/04/pra-cracks-down-on-restaurants-violating-tax-laws)). Even card acceptance has consequences, because acquiring a bank card terminal makes a retailer Tier-1 under the Sales Tax Act ([FBR POS Booklet](https://download1.fbr.gov.pk/Docs/2023919991847265Point-of-Sale(POS)-Booklet-(LegalProvision)-updated-18.09.2023.pdf)).

So the demo bill keeps the realistic structure (net prices, tax rate and amount, payment mode, sequential number) and strips everything fiscal. It must not show an FBR or provincial fiscal number, any QR code on the bill, the FBR POS logo, the Re 1 POS fee line, or the words "invoice", "fiscal", "FBR integrated", "PRA/SRB compliant", "e-invoicing ready" or "Tax Asaan verifiable". Below is a sample bill computed by code. The numbers are a worked example using Punjab's illustrative two-tier rate, and the line prices sit inside the estimated mid-range bands discussed later.

Three choices in that bill are assumptions to flag in the product, not facts. The first is whether **service charge sits inside the taxable base**: no source covers this for any province, so it should be a setting. The second is the rounding of tax to whole rupees. The third is showing the cash-versus-card difference on the bill. That line is a deliberate demo device that makes the two-tier rule visible to an owner. A real restaurant might not print it.

## The market already gives away QR ordering and KDS, but not explanations

Local competition is dense, cheap and feature-complete. Oscar offers **free POS software and card hardware**, KDS, automated KOT routing, QR dine-in ordering, FBR/PRA/SRB reporting, Foodpanda sync and offline billing. It earns from card processing at **1.2% + Rs 10 per transaction** ([Oscar](https://oscar.pk/solutions/free-pos)). Blink, which claims more than 2,000 deployments and counts OPTP, Cheezious and Crumble as customers, charges **$25–35 per branch per month** for POS with analytics and tax integration ([Blink pricing](https://www.blinkco.io/pricing/)). CORN POS runs Rs 8,000–17,500 a month and includes QR table ordering and kitchen display on higher plans. Indolj and OnlineOrder.pk sell 0%-commission own-channel ordering from about Rs 4,999 a month, and EMenu has a free QR tier ([Menuthere comparison](https://menuthere.com/best-online-ordering-systems/pakistan)). Generic FBR-integrated POS starts around Rs 2,000–3,500 a month ([eHisabKitab](https://ehisabkitab.com/pricing); [Moneypex](https://moneypex.com/pk/restaurant-pos)). Most of this comes from vendor pages, but the conclusion holds: **tax integration and QR ordering are table stakes**, and a demo cannot win by listing them.

What vendors don't sell is explanation. Their analytics appear as "reports" or "BI dashboards", and none seen offers plain-language narratives grounded in deterministic numbers. That is a weak signal drawn from marketing pages, but it lines up with Mise's architecture, where code computes every figure and the model only phrases it. The highest-value analytics for this market follow from the pain points above. They are net margin by channel (dine-in versus parcel versus own delivery versus aggregator after commission), cash-versus-digital mix and the tax difference it implies, portion mix (half versus full karahi), and the low margin of bought-in drinks and mutton. The owner complaints that are best documented concern FBR integration reliability: sync failures, "fake disconnects", and outlets sealed over unverifiable invoices. The Federal Tax Ombudsman attributed these to substandard software ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=201924)). Mise should not enter that fight in a demo. It should state plainly that real deployment requires an accredited, integrated POS alongside it.

Guest attitudes to QR ordering in Pakistan are unmeasured. The only survey data is American and unfavourable: in a Toast survey, **81% preferred a physical menu** ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/qr-code-menu-insights)). Waiters are cheap and families include older members without smartphones. Pakistan's internet penetration is only about **46%** even with 194M cellular connections ([DataReportal 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-pakistan)), and **48% of locally assembled phones in 2025 were still 2G feature phones** ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=203720)). QR ordering should therefore be pitched as an accuracy and add-on tool working alongside the waiter, with waiter entry as an equal path, not as staff replacement.

## Urdu belongs in the guest app as Naskh, RTL and Latin digits

Urdu support is a policy change. The project currently allows only `en` and `it`, and Italian has no Pakistani audience, so the locale set for this demo should become `en` + `ur`. The implementation rules are well established. Set `<html lang="ur" dir="rtl">` server-side per locale and use Tailwind's logical utilities (`ms-/me-/ps-/pe-/text-start`) instead of left/right. Wrap every left-to-right island (prices, phone numbers, table and order numbers, English dish names) in `<bdi>` or `dir="ltr"`, as W3C directs ([W3C inline bidi](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/inline-bidi-markup/)). Mirror only directional icons such as back arrows and chevrons, and leave clocks, numbers, charts and media controls unmirrored ([Material bidirectionality](https://m2.material.io/go/design-bidirectionality)). Measured ICU output for `Intl.NumberFormat('ur-PK', {currency:'PKR'})` is `Rs 1,250.00` with no direction marks, so it still needs isolation inside Urdu text.

Use a **Naskh** face for UI. Pakistan's big Urdu news sites ship Naskh for body text: Jang uses Noto Naskh Arabic, while Geo and Express use Nafees Web Naskh ([jang.com.pk](https://jang.com.pk/); [urdu.geo.tv](https://urdu.geo.tv/)). Noto Naskh Arabic is OFL-licensed and about **51 KB** (woff2, Arabic subset), against **156 KB** for Noto Nastaliq Urdu ([Google Fonts CSS API](https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Nastaliq+Urdu:wght@400..700)). Nastaliq also has documented clipping, fallback and joining problems in browsers ([W3C Urdu gap analysis](https://www.w3.org/TR/arab-ur-gap)). If Nastaliq is wanted at all, it belongs in headings only, at line-height 1.8 or more. Jameel Noori Nastaleeq is not freely licensed and must be avoided ([Wikimedia T62685](https://phabricator.wikimedia.org/T62685)). Load the Urdu font only on the `ur` route. Never style part of an Urdu word, for example with search highlighting, because it breaks cursive joining.

Use **Western digits**. CLDR's `ur-PK` defaults to `latn`, foodpanda.pk prints `Rs. 1,300` ([foodpanda.pk](https://www.foodpanda.pk/)), and browsers render Urdu-Indic digits with inconsistent glyph shapes ([W3C gap analysis](https://www.w3.org/TR/arab-ur-gap)). Format with `maximumFractionDigits: 0`, because PKR menu prices are whole rupees, and keep server-side price strings identical to the client's.

Translation needs a human. No national standard for Urdu UI terms exists, and the CRULP/NLA glossary is the only public term base ([CLE glossary](https://cle.org.pk/software/localization/OSS/ossGlossary.html)). Dish names should be transliterated as customers say them (چکن کڑاہی, not a translated "wok"), using the polite آپ register. MT output tends toward the overly informal "tum" register, which can seem disrespectful to older users ([ProZ](https://www.proz.com/doc/5138)). Allergen labels have safety consequences, so they must come from a reviewed fixed glossary rendered by server code, never from a model. Pakistani restaurants rarely label allergens at all. Punjab Food Authority's 2023 order mainly targeted hotels and clubs ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2023/07/21/punjab-restaurants-and-hotels-ordered-to-list-nutrition-information-on-food-items/amp/)), which makes Mise's deterministic allergen display a quiet differentiator rather than an expected feature.

Kitchen tickets are a separate question. Thermal ESC/POS printers generally can't shape Urdu from their code pages, so Urdu must be printed as a raster image ([pub.dev unified_esc_pos_printer](https://pub.dev/documentation/unified_esc_pos_printer/3.4.0/)). The cheap path in Mise's stack is browser printing of an HTML ticket at 58 or 80 mm, letting the OS driver rasterize. Kitchen practice is unsourced, but menus on delivery platforms use English or Roman-Urdu names such as "Chicken Karahi" and "Raita" ([Foodpanda listings](https://www.foodpanda.pk/restaurant/k3mn/hafiz-food-corner-sahiwal)). The KDS should therefore lead with the short canonical English/Roman-Urdu name, with an optional Urdu line beneath it, and use big Latin digits for table, quantity and portion.

## Devices, outages and WhatsApp set hard limits on the design

The guest is on budget Android. Android holds about **91% of mobile OS share** ([Statcounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/pakistan)), and the top local assemblers are Infinix, VGO Tel, Vivo and Itel, with a replacement cycle of about 3.5 years ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=203720)). Data is cheap at about **Rs 34 per GB** ([TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/pakistan-ranks-6th-globally-for-cheapest-mobile-data/)), so the constraint is device speed, not data cost. The guest bundle should stay small, with no heavy images, and the Urdu font should load only when needed.

The kitchen faces power and network failure. NEPRA data show high-loss feeders averaging **10–12 hours of daily outages** in Karachi ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2627009/nepra-orders-k-electric-to-end-load-shedding)). The August 2024 national slowdown cut speeds by **30–40%** ([Barchart/AP](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/28097817/pakistan-suffers-internet-slowdown-as-government-denies-setting-up-a-national-firewall)). Local vendors still advertise "works without internet" ([Oscar blog](https://oscar.pk/blog/how-does-a-restaurant-pos-system-work-in-pakistan)). Mise's demo needs no offline engine, but the KDS should show a clear connection-lost banner, keep displaying already-received tickets, and retry on reconnect. The deployment story should say that a local-network setup on a UPS is the real-world requirement.

WhatsApp is everywhere, but the API is not free. Meta has charged per message for template messages since July 2025 and raised Pakistan utility and authentication rates in April 2026 ([Meta pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)). That conflicts with the free-only rule. WhatsApp was also throttled during the 2024 disruptions ([Intellinews](https://intellinews.com/pakistan-s-national-firewall-may-lead-to-300mn-in-losses-warns-it-industry-association-339024)). The right demo role is a **"Share bill on WhatsApp" button built from a `wa.me` link** that the guest's own device opens. It costs nothing and sends no personal data to Mise or to a model. Nothing kitchen-critical should depend on it.

## Unverified assumptions to check with real owners

| Assumption | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Waiters in mid-tier desi places take orders on paper pads, not handhelds | Decides whether waiter entry in Mise is a real workflow or a demo nicety |

## Conclusion

Localizing Mise for Pakistan is mostly a data-model change and only partly a translation. Portions, payment-dependent tax and order channels have to exist in the schema before Urdu text adds any value. The tax rule is a gift for a demo: a bill that shows Rs 472 at the card rate against Rs 943 at the cash rate makes deterministic, code-computed numbers vivid to an owner in a way an Italian trattoria never could. The same rule turns careless realism into legal exposure. The line between a convincing sample bill and a counterfeit fiscal invoice is a fiscal number and a QR code, and Mise must stay clearly on the safe side of it.

Strategically, Mise should not compete as another POS in a market where POS is free. Its defensible claim is that it explains the business: it shows what Foodpanda really costs per order, how much tax a cash-heavy evening adds, and which half-karahi deal loses money. Every one of those numbers is computed by code and only phrased by a model. That positioning, and every operational assumption above, should be tested in a handful of owner interviews in Lahore and Islamabad before any feature beyond the P0 data model is built.
